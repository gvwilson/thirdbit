Closing Time


Legal and Administrative Matters

Why This Matters

  • Legal and administrative tasks are the most commonly skipped and the most likely to cause problems later
  • Most important: keep a record of decisions made during shutdown
    • What was transferred, to whom, and when
    • Make sure that record is available after shutdown

Key Legal Checks

  • Determine who legally owns the project's outputs
    • Individual contributors, an institution, or a formal entity
    • IP policies differ — check, don't assume
  • Open source licenses are irrevocable, but closing repositories is still meaningful for your community
  • Confidential and personally identifiable data has specific legal obligations for retention and deletion

Key Legal Checks

  • Trademarks, domain names, and social media handles are assets with legal owners
    • Transfer or release them explicitly
  • Financial accounts require formal closure; residual funds may need donation or return
  • Grant reporting obligations survive project shutdown

Be Proactive

  • Build exit clauses into contracts and development agreements from the start, not when you need them
  • The difference between rules as written and rules as enforced matters most during institutional turmoil
  • Check whether a final grant report is due — reporting obligations survive shutdown

  1. Working individually, list the legal or administrative obligations that arise when this project shuts down.
  2. Put them in the order they must be addressed. Which obligations create deadlines that constrain everything else?
  3. Compare with a partner.
