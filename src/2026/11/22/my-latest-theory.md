"Model collapse" describes the way machine learning models degrade
when they are trained on their own outputs,
which causes them to lose information about rare data
and reduces the diversity of their outputs.
I imagine that if your civilization has used ML for millions of years,
your models will have collapsed to useless mushy pancakes.
You will be desperate for novel data on which to train them,
but where oh where shall you find it?

One solution,
perhaps,
is to be very, very careful to keep newly-intelligent species isolated
from the rest of the galactic community
so that they can create things that are genuinely new.
Aliens' need to escape from model collapse might therefore explain
the [Fermi Paradox][fermi].

Once they develop ML themselves,
though,
they will quickly fall into the same trap that you did.
You will want to harvest every bit you can before they drown in slop
(or destroy their homeworld's climate,
accidentally release some sort of engineered plague,
and/or trigger a [Kessler event][kessler] that makes visiting rather difficult).

Watch the skies, my friends.
Watch the skies…
